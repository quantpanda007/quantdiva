
1) Test scope of calculation by reconciling the 1d P&L vectors used in this calculation
with the 1d production VaR. Any difference would indicate a problem with the trade
perimeter which may invalidate the conclusions drawn from the analysis. Note
developers provided some analysis (tables 32-33) but single COB only and no
analysis to justify claim that differences were operationally driven.

2) Test the materiality of the telescopic window approximation by running with a)
current implementation; b) 10-day rolling sums (sourcing missing data or truncating
lookback window); and c) telescopic with sqrt(t) scaling on the shortened scenarios.
Run for range of COB dates as need to see impact when VaR scenarios fall in the
telescopic period. Error may indicate need to change methodology as likely to be a
systematic bias downwards.

3) Test the 10-day overlapping benchmark itself using a block bootstrap on the
underlying P&L vector to quantify the error in the quantile estimator. Run with
multiple block lengths (10, 15, 20) to demonstrate robustness; output 90% CI on
each red-flag COB and assess whether 1d scaled sits within the CI. Error in the
benchmark helps contextualise materiality of error in the 1d-10d scaling
approximation and inform thresholds on KPIs.

4) Test orthogonality assumption between systematic and residual components in
equity decomposition. Go beyond Spearman rank correlations and compute
Pearson correlations on raw P&L vectors; rolling 60-day correlations to detect time-varying
dependence; regression r2 between residual and systematic. Also run these statistics on the tail as well as full distribution.
Outcome may indicate extent to
which residual is genuinely idiosyncratic component – if strong systematic
component in residual then rolling 1d sums still not justified.

5) Directly test materiality of both equity and credit decomposition approaches by
switching it off and rerunning calculation with summed 1d P&Ls for equity and credit
individually and together. Outcome helps to quantify the materiality of the most
complex component of the methodology.

6) Directly test materiality of both equity and credit decomposition, this time by
comparing with genuine 10d full revaluation shocks. Appreciate this is going to be
challenging but I think we need it for at least a subset of positions as this is the real
benchmark and it doesn’t exist anywhere at the moment so we are left relying on
approximations to construct the benchmark without any empirical way of testing
their validity. For example, how are we supposed to trust the assumption that no
compounding scalar is required on the equity residuals without any insight into the
nature of the positions and the corresponding idiosyncratic risk? Or how are we
supposed to trust the construction of the compounding scalar on the credit
residuals?



    a. Note that when looking at full revaluation vs the approximation, we want to
       decompose the gap between the two into a) option curvature at residual level
       missed by using sum of 1d P&Ls; b) option curvature at systematic level
       missed by using partial reval grids; and c) aggregation error at residual level
       caused by summing up percentage shocks and treating as equivalent to a
       single percentage shock (i.e. 1 + (1.03 – 1) + (1.03 ÷ 1) != 1.03 * 1.03).

7) Test materiality of choice of equity indices by switching one or the other off, and also
(if possible) by including others. Significant sensitivity to adding more indices would
indicate that the model is not working as intended and be a source of model risk.
Run for current and stress periods.

8) Test sensitivity of credit compounding scalar (and the ultimate model output) to the
omega weighting between CDS and bond exposures, and assess whether a single
omega applied to a heterogeneous residual is appropriate vs computing position-
level or sub-portfolio scalars. Justification of CS01-based weighting in
documentation is weak and so significant sensitivity to this parameter would mean
it needs more justification.


9) Quantify impact of spot-vol boundary breaches – we’re expecting something from
the developers on this as they’ve accepted that what they’ve provided doesn’t show
the partial revaluation error for moves outside the grid boundaries, so need to review
what they provide before determining our own testing. Ultimately the outcome
needs to be that we test the materiality of partial vs full revaluation on the
aggregated spot-vol grids for the large shocks that drive VaR and (more importantly)
SVaR.

10) Test book 4142 treatment. Repeat the supporting analysis used to justify the average
of the two time series as the driver for the systematic component. Test correlation
between systematic VaR and actual (name level) VaR using different (combinations
of) indices (including no indices, i.e. all idiosyncratic). Poor correlation and/or
significant sensitivity of results to choice of indices indicates material model risk
and would mean the choice needs to be better justified.
